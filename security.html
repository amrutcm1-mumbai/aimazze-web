How AIMAZZE Protects Your Data

Last updated: July 22, 2026

AI is already showing up in regulated, privileged work — often through tools nobody vetted. The risk isn't AI itself. It's not knowing which tier of AI you're using, and what that tier actually does with your data. Here's the honest breakdown.

Tier 1 — Local Models: Private by Architecture

Local models run entirely on your own hardware. There is no network call, no server round-trip, no third party in the loop. Nothing about your prompts, documents, or outputs is transmitted — not to AIMAZZE, not to any AI provider, not to anyone.

This is the only tier where privilege and confidentiality are protected structurally, not contractually. If your work involves attorney-client privilege, patient data, or anything you legally cannot risk exposing, this is the tier built for that constraint.

Trade-off, stated honestly: local models are typically smaller and less capable than the largest cloud models, and require enough local hardware to run well.

Tier 2 — Bring Your Own Key (BYOK): Device-to-Provider

When you connect your own API key from a provider (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, etc.), your requests travel directly from your device to that provider using your credentials. AIMAZZE is never in that data path — we don't receive, log, or store what you send.

But that is not the same as blanket privacy. Once your data reaches the provider, their data-handling terms apply — not ours. Some providers exclude API traffic from model training by default; others don't, or only do so under specific account settings. This is genuinely worth 10 minutes of reading before you send anything sensitive through this tier.

What we recommend: check your provider's API terms (not their consumer app's terms — these often differ) for data retention and training-use language before using this tier for privileged or regulated work.

Tier 3 — Public Consumer AI Tools: Proceed with Caution

Free, general-purpose consumer AI apps and browser extensions are built for broad usefulness, not confidentiality. Many retain conversation data, use it to improve their models, or share it with affiliated services — often disclosed only in terms most people never read.

This is the tier that's created real exposure for professionals handling privileged or regulated information, and the pattern regulators and courts are increasingly paying attention to. If a task involves anything privileged, regulated, or client-identifiable, this tier is the one to avoid.

The bottom line

AIMAZZE doesn't ask you to trust a single privacy claim. It gives you the choice — local when the work demands it, your own keys when you want cloud capability without a middleman, and clear-eyed information either way. You decide which tier fits which task.
